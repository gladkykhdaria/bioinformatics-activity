Exercise 1: gel electrophoresis

  1. Take the samples

    For each sample pick a random length between 100 and 3000 (not longer than the sequence) and a random start, then copy that part. All 10 go into one array.

    CS: random numbers + list of strings

  2. Why DNA moves

    DNA has a negative charge, so in an electric field it moves to the + side. The gel works like a net: short pieces get through easily, long pieces get stuck more.

  3. How far it moves

    Distance is not linear with size, it follows the logarithm: distance = a − b · log10(length). That's why 100 and 200 bp are far apart on a gel, but 2000 and 2100 bp almost touch.

    CS: one math function per fragment

  4. Read the size

    The ladder lane has fragments of known size. Compare a band with the ladder next to it to estimate its length, like reading a ruler.

Exercise 2: tandem repeats

  1. What we look for

    A short unit of 3–5 bases written two or more times in a row, so that the whole block is 6–10 bases. For example ATG ATG, ATG ATG ATG, GATC GATC, ACGTT ACGTT.

  2. How the scan works

    For unit length k, compare every letter with the letter k places later. A row of matches means the sequence is repeating with period k. Region length = matches + k, and copies = region ÷ k.

    CS: one pass per k, O(n)

  3. Skip fake units

    AAA is really just A repeated, and ATAT is AT repeated. These aren't real 3–5 base units, so they're skipped.

  4. Compare with chance

    Short repeats also show up in random DNA by pure luck. The app shuffles the same letters 100 times and counts again. If the real count is inside that range, the repeats are mostly random.

    CS: shuffle test (a simple permutation test)
