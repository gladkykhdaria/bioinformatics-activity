What a transposon is

A piece of DNA that can cut itself out and jump to another place in the genome. A transposase enzyme recognises its two ends, which are inverted repeats: the right end is the left end read backwards on the other strand.

How it gets inserted (the board picture)

1. Host DNA, the target site is cut on both strands, a few bases apart ...TAG ATGCA CAG... ...ATC TACGT GTC... 2. Transposon goes into the cut, leaving single-stranded gaps ...TAG ATGCA [IR transposon IR] CAG... ...ATC [IR transposon IR] TACGT GTC... 3. The cell fills in the gaps, so the target site is now on both sides ...TAG ATGCA [IR transposon IR] ATGCA CAG... ...ATC TACGT [IR transposon IR] TACGT GTC... direct repeat direct repeat

How the detector works

  1. Index every 7-letter word

    Save each 7-letter word with its positions in a dictionary.

    CS: hash table

  2. Look for inverted repeat pairs

    For each word, compute its reverse complement (reverse it and swap A↔T, C↔G) and look it up. A match 24–150 letters to the right is a possible transposon.

    CS: lookup instead of comparing every pair

  3. Grow the match

    Extend outwards and inwards while the letters are still reverse complements. This gives the full inverted repeat.

  4. Check the direct repeats

    The 4–10 letters just before the start must be the same as the letters just after the end. If not, move both edges one letter inwards and try again. Random letters next to the IR sometimes fake one extra matching letter, and this step fixes that.

  5. Remove overlaps and report

    If two candidates overlap, keep the one with the higher score (IR length + 2 × direct repeat length). Report start and end, counting from 1.

Why random DNA almost never fools it

A 7-letter word has 47 = 16,384 versions. In 400 letters with a 125-letter window we expect about 400 × 125 ÷ 16,384 ≈ 3 random IR pairs. Each one also needs a 4-letter direct repeat in exactly the right place, which has a chance of 1 in 44 = 256. So about 3 ÷ 256 ≈ 0.01 false transposons per sequence.
